TITLE:
Offline voiceprint-guided three-dimensional acoustic localization of drones with a six-microphone rigid spherical array: rotor silence windows and reference subtraction

ABSTRACT:
This study investigates three-dimensional acoustic localization of drones with a 160 mm rigid sphere carrying six microphones on its poles, in simulation and with real single-channel drone recordings propagated synthetically to the array. The simulator models rigid-sphere scattering, ISO 9613-1 absorption, distributed rotor noise, rotor spin-down and hardware errors. In an active mode the target emits a 3–6 kHz chirp; with 0.2 s rotor silence windows, matched filtering and sphere-steered SRP-PHAT, 25/30 closed-loop approaches from 80 m succeeded, against 0/30 with motors running and 8/30 with an ego-noise reference canceller; an optimistic coherent-rotor model raises the last figure to 29/30, so whether the windows are needed depends on a measurable reference coherence. In a passive mode a station locates the arriving drone from its own rotor noise: reference subtraction, a blade-passing-frequency drone-type signature and sphere-steered, harmonic-weighted SRP-PHAT gave a median direction error of 1.13° over 30 randomized approaches. Leakage-free evaluation lowered signature accuracy to 72% across real recording campaigns. On real drone-embedded recordings (DREGON, eight-microphone array, free flight at signal-to-noise ratios near −12 dB), the same steered-response-power estimator with subtraction of the average noise cross-spectra located a broadband loudspeaker within 10° in 182/217 segments (median 3.4°), whereas in-flight speech was not localized. All processing is offline: the chain was trained and run with every network connection blocked, needs no internet, cloud service or satellite positioning, and kept pace with real time on one CPU core. Validation of the spherical sensor itself is in progress.
